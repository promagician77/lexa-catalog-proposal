How I'd run it

The goal is simple: nothing lives in someone's head or inbox. Every SKU has a row, every gap has an owner, and every owner hears from me until it's closed.

  1. Audit and build the master trackerPull every product from Shopify and the catalog portal into one Google Sheet. Mark what's missing for each SKU: info, images, CAD, size chart, factory check. You get a gap report at the end of week one.
  2. Agree the standards onceProduct naming, categories, image specs, and file names, signed off by you before anything is uploaded. That stops rework later.
  3. Run three tracks in parallelThe CAD designer works through a prioritized queue, factories confirm specs and size charts, and I upload finished SKUs to Shopify in batches.
  4. Test as a customerBrowse by sport, open every product, check sizes, images, and links on phone and desktop. Anything wrong goes back on the tracker.
  5. Launch, then keep it cleanA short checklist for adding new products, so the catalog stays complete after launch.

First look at your site

A few things I noticed on your public Product Details page. Small, but they're exactly the kind of detail this project is about.

One fabric, three names

The to90 fabric appears as "to90" in the heading, "T090 Fabrix" in the image description, and "0_TO90" in the file name. Customers searching, and your own team, won't always know these are the same thing.

Fix: one official name per fabric, used everywhere.

Capitalization varies

"Butterfly mesh" sits next to "Recycled Cool Dry" and "Cool Dry". A small thing, but it's what makes a catalog look finished or unfinished.

Fix: a naming rule applied in one pass.

Images without descriptions

Several logo and enhancement images, like the patch and embroidery photos, have no alt text. That affects accessibility and how search engines read the page.

Fix: a short description for every image during upload.
